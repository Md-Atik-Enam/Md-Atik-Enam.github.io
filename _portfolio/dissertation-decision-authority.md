  <section class="case-section case-section--tight">
    <h2>Project Objective</h2>
    <p>AI can influence a decision before it recommends an answer. It can decide how a person's request is interpreted, which factors matter, and which options become available. Giving a user the final choice does not remove this earlier influence.</p>
    <p>The project examined two layers of authority: who defines the decision space and who acts within that space.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="case-section">
    <h2>AURA Prototype</h2>
    <p>AURA turns a passenger's natural-language route request into an interactive set of decision factors and route options. The prototype made it possible to change who could shape the decision representation and who could make the final route choice.</p>
    <div class="case-timeline" aria-label="AURA decision process">
      <div><strong>1. Express intent</strong><span>The passenger describes what matters in natural language.</span></div>
      <div><strong>2. Build decision space</strong><span>AURA translates the request into structured route factors.</span></div>
      <div><strong>3. Review options</strong><span>The passenger or AI can revise, approve, or negotiate the factors.</span></div>
      <div><strong>4. Select route</strong><span>The passenger, AI, or both determine the final route.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="case-section">
    <h2>Method Alternatives and Choice</h2>
    <div class="case-method-grid case-method-grid--choices">
      <div>
        <h3>Alternative: Final-choice experiment only</h3>
        <p>Testing only who selected the final route would measure downstream control, but it would miss the earlier authority used to define the factors and options in the decision space.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Alternative: Survey vignettes</h3>
        <p>Vignettes could compare many situations quickly, but they would provide limited behavioral realism because participants would not build, inspect, revise, or negotiate an actual route decision.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Alternative: Interviews only</h3>
        <p>Interviews would provide rich explanations of agency and authorship, but they would not isolate how upstream and downstream authority changed choices and perceptions.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="is-chosen">
        <h3>Chosen: Prototype, experiments, and interviews</h3>
        <p>This was the best fit because the working AURA prototype made authority visible and manipulable, the experiments tested effects, and interviews explained how participants understood control and authorship.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="case-section">
    <h2>Three Connected Studies</h2>
    <div class="case-method-grid">

      <div>
        <h3>Study B: Upstream authority</h3>
        <p>Tested whether people could inspect and revise how AURA represented their request.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Study C: Authority in practice</h3>
        <p>Used interviews to examine how participants understood influence, control, and authorship.</p>
      </div>

  <section class="case-section">
    <h2>Two Layers of Authority</h2>
    <div class="case-insight-grid">
      <article>
        <span class="case-tag">Upstream</span>
        <h3>Authority over the decision space</h3>
        <p>Who decides how the user's request is translated into goals, constraints, and options?</p>
      </article>
      <article>
        <span class="case-tag case-tag--blue">Downstream</span>
        <h3>Authority within the decision space</h3>
        <p>Who compares the available options, negotiates tradeoffs, and makes the final choice?</p>
      </article>
      <article>
        <span class="case-tag case-tag--amber">Authorship</span>
        <h3>Whose decision does it feel like?</h3>
        <p>People may still experience authorship when they recognize their intentions in the AI's contribution.</p>
      </article>
      <article>
        <span class="case-tag case-tag--rose">Recourse</span>
        <h3>What can the user reopen?</h3>
        <p>Meaningful control may require revising the AI's earlier interpretation, not only changing the final option.</p>
      </article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="case-section">
    <h2>Key Findings</h2>
    <div class="case-metric-grid">
      <div><strong>Decision paths changed</strong><span>Different downstream arrangements changed how people moved through the decision.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Route outcomes changed</strong><span>The authority structure was linked to different final route choices.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Control was layered</strong><span>More upstream authority increased perceived structural authority.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Authorship was not automatic</strong><span>More control did not always increase the feeling that the final decision belonged to the user.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="case-section">
    <h2>Design Recommendations</h2>
    <div class="case-recommendations">
      <div>
        <h3>Show how the request was interpreted</h3>
        <p>Let users inspect the goals, constraints, and assumptions created from their input.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Support upstream correction</h3>
        <p>Allow users to revise the decision representation before comparing final options.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Make authority transitions visible</h3>
        <p>Clearly show when the AI recommends, negotiates, decides, or returns control.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Measure authorship separately from control</h3>
        <p>Ask whether users recognize their intentions in the outcome, not only whether they clicked the final button.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="case-section">
    <h2>Broader Product Relevance</h2>
    <p>The framework applies beyond route planning. AI tools in healthcare, finance, hiring, education, and productivity also shape how a problem is represented before offering a recommendation. Product teams should give users ways to inspect and revise that earlier influence.</p>
